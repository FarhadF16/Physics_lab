Modern Physics

Modern physics explores phenomena that cannot be fully explained by classical physics. It includes relativity, quantum mechanics, atomic physics, nuclear physics, and particle physics.


Relativity

Einstein's theory of special relativity changed how physicists understand space, time, motion, and energy. Measurements of time and distance can depend on the relative motion of observers.


Mass and Energy

Relativity also shows that mass and energy are closely connected. Matter can be associated with enormous amounts of energy because mass is a form of stored energy.


Quantum Physics

Quantum physics describes nature at very small scales, including atoms, electrons, and photons. At this scale, particles can behave in ways that are very different from everyday objects.


Wave-Particle Duality

Quantum objects can display both wave-like and particle-like properties. Experiments with light and matter demonstrate that classical descriptions alone are not always sufficient.


The Atom

Atoms consist of a nucleus surrounded by electrons. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. The behavior of electrons in atoms is described using quantum physics.


Energy Levels

Electrons in atoms can occupy specific energy states. When an electron changes between energy levels, the atom can absorb or emit electromagnetic radiation.


Nuclear Physics

Nuclear physics studies atomic nuclei and the forces and processes that affect them. Nuclear reactions can release or absorb large amounts of energy.


Radioactivity

Some atomic nuclei are unstable and naturally transform into more stable states. During radioactive decay, particles or electromagnetic radiation can be emitted.


Particle Physics

Particle physics investigates the fundamental particles that make up matter and the forces through which they interact. It helps scientists study the basic structure of the universe.


Key Ideas

Modern physics expands our understanding of nature from the smallest particles to the behavior of space and time. Relativity and quantum mechanics are two of its most important foundations.
