Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics studies heat, temperature, energy, and how energy moves between systems. It helps explain everything from engines and refrigerators to weather and changes of state.


Temperature

Temperature measures the average kinetic energy associated with the microscopic motion of particles in a substance. Higher temperature generally means particles have greater average kinetic energy.


Heat

Heat is energy transferred between objects or systems because of a temperature difference. Heat naturally moves from warmer regions toward cooler regions until thermal equilibrium is reached.


Thermal Energy

Thermal energy is associated with the microscopic motion and interactions of particles within a system. It can change when energy is transferred into or out of the system.


Methods of Heat Transfer

Conduction transfers thermal energy through direct contact. Convection transfers thermal energy through the movement of fluids. Radiation transfers energy through electromagnetic waves and does not require a material medium.


Changes of State

Matter can change between solid, liquid, and gas states. Melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, and boiling involve energy transfers between a substance and its surroundings.


First Law of Thermodynamics

The first law is a statement of energy conservation applied to thermodynamic systems. Energy can be transferred as heat or work, but the total energy is conserved.


Second Law of Thermodynamics

The second law describes the direction of natural thermodynamic processes. It is closely connected to entropy and explains why energy transfers have preferred directions.


Heat Engines

A heat engine converts some thermal energy into useful mechanical work. Engines are limited by thermodynamic principles, so not all supplied thermal energy can be converted into useful work.


Key Ideas

Thermodynamics connects heat, temperature, energy, work, and entropy. Its laws help explain how energy moves and why real systems behave in predictable ways.
