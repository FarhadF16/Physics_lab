Electricity & Magnetism

Electricity and magnetism describe how electric charges and magnetic fields interact. Together, they form one of the major areas of classical physics.


Electric Charge

Electric charge is a property of matter. There are two types of charge: positive and negative. Like charges repel each other, while opposite charges attract.


Electric Force

Charged objects exert forces on one another. The strength of the electric force depends on the amount of charge and the distance between the charges.


Electric Fields

An electric field describes the influence a charge would experience at a particular location. Electric field lines can be used to visualize the direction and relative strength of a field.


Electric Current

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a circuit. Current is measured in amperes, or amps.


Voltage

Voltage describes the difference in electric potential between two points. A voltage difference can provide the energy needed to move charges through a circuit.


Resistance

Resistance describes how strongly a material opposes the flow of electric current. Different materials and components can have very different resistances.


Ohm's Law

Ohm's law describes the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in many electrical components. It is commonly written as V = IR.


Circuits

A circuit provides a complete path for electric current. Components can be connected in series or parallel, producing different behaviors for current and voltage.


Magnetism

Magnets produce magnetic fields that can interact with other magnets, magnetic materials, and moving electric charges.


Electromagnetism

Electricity and magnetism are closely connected. Moving electric charges can produce magnetic fields, while changing magnetic fields can induce electric effects. This relationship is the foundation of technologies such as motors and generators.


Key Ideas

Electricity involves charge, current, voltage, and resistance. Magnetism involves magnetic fields and their interactions. Together, electricity and magnetism explain many of the technologies used every day.
