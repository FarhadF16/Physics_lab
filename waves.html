Waves & Optics

Waves transfer energy from one place to another. They appear in many parts of everyday life, including sound, light, water waves, and communication systems.


What Is a Wave?

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy through a medium or through space. In general, the material itself does not travel with the wave over long distances.


Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

In a transverse wave, particles or fields oscillate perpendicular to the direction the wave travels. In a longitudinal wave, the oscillations occur parallel to the direction of travel.


Wavelength and Frequency

Wavelength is the distance between corresponding points on successive waves. Frequency describes how many complete cycles occur each second and is measured in hertz (Hz).


Wave Speed

Wave speed depends on frequency and wavelength. Changing either of these quantities can change the speed relationship of a wave.


Sound Waves

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium such as air, water, or a solid to travel. The frequency of a sound is related to its pitch, while its amplitude is related to how intense the sound is.


Light

Light is electromagnetic radiation and can travel through empty space. Visible light is only a small portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.


Reflection

Reflection occurs when a wave reaches a surface and changes direction. Mirrors are a common example of reflection involving visible light.


Refraction

Refraction occurs when a wave changes direction because its speed changes as it enters a different medium. Lenses use refraction to control the path of light.


Diffraction and Interference

Diffraction is the spreading of waves around obstacles or through openings. Interference occurs when waves overlap and combine, producing patterns of constructive or destructive interference.


Key Ideas

Waves transfer energy and can reflect, refract, diffract, and interfere. Understanding these behaviors helps explain sound, light, lenses, communication, and many optical technologies.
